## Team Memo Grading (40 points) 

## 1. Reproducibility & Technical Rigor (10 points) 

## **Excellent (9-10 points):** 

- ✅ **Code runs end-to-end:** All scripts from M1, M2, M3 execute without errors 

- ✅ **Results match memo:** Tables and figures in memo can be regenerated from code 

- ✅ **Models are correct:** FE and DiD/ARIMA/ML specifications are sound 

- ✅ **Diagnostics complete:** All assumption tests run; violations addressed 

- ✅ **Robustness checks:** At least 3 checks conducted and results compared 

## **Satisfactory (6-8 points):** 

- ✅ Code runs with minor warnings (not critical errors) 

- ✅ Most results match, but 1-2 tables/figures have minor discrepancies 

- ✅ Models mostly correct, but 1-2 specification issues 

- ✅ Diagnostics run but not all violations addressed 

## **Needs Improvement (3-5 points):** 

- ⚠ Code requires manual debugging to run 

- ⚠ Significant discrepancies between code output and memo tables 

- ⚠ Model specifications have errors 

- ⚠ Minimal diagnostics or robustness checks 

## **Unsatisfactory (0-2 points):** 

- ❌ Code does not run at all 

- ❌ Results in memo cannot be reproduced 

- ❌ Models are fundamentally flawed

## **Interpretation (6 points):** 

## **Excellent (6 points):** 

- ✅ **Coefficient interpretation:** "A 1 pp increase in FEDFUNDS reduces REIT returns by 2.5 pp" (economically clear) 

- ✅ **Magnitude assessed:** "During 2022-2023 rate hike (5.25 pp), model predicts 13% decline" 

- ✅ **Economic mechanisms explained:** Leverage channel, discount rate channel, demand effects

- ⚠ No robustness discussion

## Frequently Asked Questions 

## Q: Can we use bullet points in the Executive Summary? 

**A:** No. Executive Summary should be 2-3 paragraphs of prose (no tables, figures, or bullet lists). It's designed to be read by busy executives who want a quick narrative summary. 

## Q: Our memo is 8 pages. Will we lose points? 

**A:** Likely yes. If it's 8 pages with essential content, minor penalty (1-2 points). If it's 8 pages with fluff, more significant penalty (3-5 points). **Target: 5-7 pages.** 

Q: We disagree on investment recommendations. How do we handle this? 

9 / 10 

**A:** Present the recommendation the team agreed on by majority vote. If disagreement is genuine, you can note it: "Our team considered both overweighting and neutral stance on Retail REITs. We chose neutral due to [X], but acknowledge that an overweight strategy could be defensible given [Y]." 

**Individual Addendum:** If you personally disagree, you can note this in your "Defended Decision" section and explain your alternative reasoning. 

## Q: Can we include our code in an appendix? 

**A:** No. The memo is for the Investment Committee (non-technical). Code should be in your GitHub repo, not in the PDF memo. **Exception:** Short code snippets (2-3 lines) in footnotes to explain a methodological choice, but this is rare. 

## Q: What if we ran out of time and didn't polish the memo? 

**A:** You'll lose points for Structure & Clarity (typos, formatting errors) and potentially Recommendations (if rushed and vague). **Time management is part of the assessment.** Start the memo during the Week 14 hackathon; submit by **11:59 PM Friday, May 1** (end of Week 14). 

## Grading Philosophy 

## **This rubric emphasizes:** 

1. **Professional communication:** Can you translate technical analysis into actionable business advice? 

2. **Intellectual honesty:** Do you acknowledge limitations and uncertainty? 

3. **Evidence-based recommendations:** Are your investment suggestions grounded in empirical findings? 

4. **Individual accountability:** Did you contribute meaningfully and reflect thoughtfully? 

## **We are not grading:** 

- Technical sophistication of models (that was M3) 

- Length of memo (concise is better than verbose) Creative visualizations (functional is fine) 

## **We are grading:** 

Can a non-economist understand your findings? 

- Are your recommendations specific and defensible? 

- Did you honestly assess strengths and limitations?
